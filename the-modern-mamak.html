The ModernMamak

This research investigates the socio-spatial role of Singapore's traditional mamak shops, small provision stores that have long served as important neighbourhood gathering spaces, and explores how they can adapt to changing social and economic conditions while preserving their cultural and community significance. Through spatial analysis, fieldwork, interviews, and demographic mapping, the study examines the evolving role of these neighbourhood anchors and identifies opportunities for community-driven revitalisation.

My involvement included conducting in-depth interviews with store owners and customers, coding and analysing qualitative data to identify key themes and patterns, and translating the findings into design strategies that support the long-term resilience and relevance of mamak shops.

Project Details.

1. Mamak Stores Throughout the Years.

First opened by migrants from Sri Lanka, India, and China, mamak stores provided everyday goods and traditional spices while doubling as vital social gathering points. Their gradual disappearance signals more than economic loss—it reflects a fading sense of local community and identity.

2. Current Efforts to Revive and Revitalize Mamak Stores.

While current initiatives aim to support small retailers through digitalization, mentorship, and placemaking programs, they often operate at a macro scale with limited focus on the shops themselves. There remains a lack of ground-up, design-driven strategies that leverage architecture and socio-spatial design to enhance the relevance and competitiveness of small provision shops. This research addresses that gap by exploring how community participation and spatial interventions can revitalize these local businesses while strengthening neighborhood identity and inclusiveness.

3. Research Methodology.

Spatial Data Analysis

Mapping of mamak stores from the 1980s to today to analyze growth and decline.

Semi-Structured Interviews

Conducted interviews with current and former store owners to understand their experiences, sentiments toward their work, and the challenges they face.

Intercept Street Survey

Conducted short surveys with residents and customers to understand their perceptions of mamak stores, focusing on their roles within the community and the dynamics between store owners and residents.

Observation (pre / post interview)

Observe and analyze the influence of socio-spatial factors shape interactions among different groups within and around provision stores.

Online Survey

Collected response from participants through an online survey to examine public experiences, preferences and perceptions of mamak shops and their significance in Singapore.

4. Key Findings.

The research findings are categorized into four topics.

Mamak Shops Remain Vital Community Hubs

"The only selling point is quality -- service quality. That means customer don't want to wait. You have item here, I need it now… the time is important for them."

Mamak Shops Face Socio-Economic Pressures

"It used to be residents around here in their 40s, in their 50s and 60s coming over to buy things…do the cooking for their household. But now this group of consumers has become lesser."" lesser."

Mamak Shops Must Innovate to Remain Relevant

"We do some stuff online…we talk online [on TikTok] about what we sell and how much we're selling. And some people come down."

Mamak Shops Reimagined as Multi-functional Community Spaces

"We have quite a number of neighbors that they will come here and have a birthday celebrations, you know, to come over to have a drink in the evening after their work."

Spatial Limitations of Current Mamak Stores

  1. Spaces tend to be dim and stuffy
  2. Lack of seating for residents to sit and interact
  3. Unorganized -- items are difficult locate
  4. Narrow circulation space -- difficult to accommodate multiple people inside at once
  5. Limited space -- lack of inventory and shop space, items end up piling outside

5. Potential Intervention.

Mamak stores can be revitalized as inclusive, multi-functional community hubs. These are spaces that blend open, flexible design with digital tools and social initiatives to strengthen local identity and collective ownership.
